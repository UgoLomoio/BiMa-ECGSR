**Figure 1 | Architecture and experimental workflow.**
**a**, Data. Three public 12-lead cohorts (PTB-XL v1.0.3, Germany, 21,799 records; Georgia 12-lead, USA, 10,344 records; Chapman-Shaoxing/Ningbo, China, 45,152 records) pass through one identical pipeline: 12 leads at 500 Hz, first 10 s; zero-phase third-order Butterworth band-pass, 0.5–40 Hz; two non-overlapping 5-s windows (2,500 samples); z-score per window and lead, with the per-lead mean and s.d. stored so that inter-lead amplitude ratios can be restored. Windows containing NaN or a flat lead (s.d. < 0.005 mV) are excluded. PTB-XL stratified folds 1–8 form the training set (34,833 windows, 17,417 records), fold 9 the validation set (4,366 windows, 2,183 records; a fixed subset of 1,024 windows is used for model selection) and fold 10 the test set (4,396 windows, 2,198 records). Georgia (20,547 windows, 10,275 records) and China (87,105 windows, 43,559 records) are external test-only cohorts, never used for training or model selection. Bar length is proportional to the number of windows.
**b**, Input-lead configurations. Pink, measured input leads; white, leads predicted by the model; hatched, predicted leads that are also exactly derivable from leads I and II by Einthoven's and Goldberger's relations (III = II − I, aVR = −(I + II)/2, aVL = I − II/2, aVF = II − I/2). The predicted-lead count includes the hatched leads. Rows are ordered by the number of measured leads: I, II, I+II, I+II+V1 (5-electrode bedside telemetry), I+II+V2 and I+II+V1+V4 (11, 11, 10, 9, 9 and 8 predicted leads). BiMa-SR is trained for all six configurations; DiBiMa-SR is trained for lead II.
**c**, Models. BiMa-SR (regression) and DiBiMa-SR (diffusion) share one backbone: a convolutional encoder (kernel 5, 64 channels), a bidirectional Mamba stack (Mamba v1; 4 blocks × 8 layers; d_model 64, d_state 16; forward and reversed scans) and a convolutional decoder (12 output channels) with an encoder–decoder skip. A lead residual adds each input lead to its own output channel. The k input leads are divided by one common scale s, where s² is the mean variance of the input leads; this preserves their amplitude ratios and equals the z-score when k = 1. The BiMa-SR encoder receives the k input leads. The DiBiMa-SR encoder receives [x_t (12 channels), k input leads], plus a sinusoidal timestep embedding and MLP added after the encoder. DiBiMa-SR is a DDPM with T = 20, a cosine schedule rescaled to zero terminal SNR and x₀-prediction. At t = 19, √ᾱ = 0, so sampling starts from pure Gaussian noise. Inset: √ᾱ_t (signal, solid) and √(1 − ᾱ_t) (noise, dashed), with dots at the DDIM-6 steps. Two samplers are used: one-step (a single x₀ prediction at t = 19) and DDIM-6 (η = 0; t = 19, 16, 12, 9, 6, 2). At evaluation, the measured input channels of the output are replaced by the true inputs. Parameters: BiMa-SR 2.68 M (2,675,148 for k = 1; 2,675,788 for k = 3; 2,676,108 for k = 4); DiBiMa-SR 2.69 M (2,687,308, k = 1). All models are trained for 20 epochs with AdamW (learning rate 10⁻³, cosine decay to 10⁻⁵; weight decay 10⁻⁶), effective batch 32 (4 micro-batches of 8), bf16, gradient clipping at 1 and an MSE loss on the 12-lead target. The checkpoint with the highest pooled median Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) over predicted leads on the 1,024 validation windows is retained.
**d**, Evaluation. Reconstructions of the PTB-XL test set, Georgia and China are assessed in two ways. (1) Reconstruction accuracy: pooled median PCC and mean absolute error (MAE) over the predicted leads, also reported separately for predicted limb and precordial leads, per lead and per diagnostic class. Comparators are a per-lead linear finite-impulse-response (FIR) filter from the input leads (101 taps per input lead; float64 least squares on 4,000 PTB-XL training windows) and, when I and II are inputs, the exact Einthoven/Goldberger limb leads. (2) Diagnostic readability: one 12-lead classifier (1D-ResNet, 3.36 M parameters) is trained only on real PTB-XL 12-lead ECGs (folds 1–8, early stopping on fold 9). It is then applied unchanged to real and to reconstructed 12-lead ECGs. AUROC is computed per record, the record probability being the mean over its two windows. The labels are the five PTB-XL superclasses (NORM, normal; MI, myocardial infarction; STTC, ST/T change; CD, conduction disturbance; HYP, hypertrophy) and a 12-label rhythm/morphology panel shared with Georgia and China (AFIB, AFLT, 1AVB, PAC, PVC, SBRAD, STACH, WPW, LVH, RVH, CRBBB, CLBBB). AFLT is not scored in China. Statistics: 95% record-bootstrap confidence intervals (B = 1,000) and paired comparisons on shared bootstrap resamples. This figure shows methods only; source data are in source_data/Figure1_*.csv.
